ABSTRACT

Chiral metamaterials have attracted great interests in recent years owing to fascinating properties such as negative refraction, strong optical activity, and circular dichroism, which can be applied in many optoelectronic devices. Helix is especially suitable for studying chiral responses as the helical geometry well resembles the feature of circularly polarized light. In this study, we use a helix structure as a model system to demonstrate the general response of circularly polarized light in a three-dimensional dielectric helix nanostructure. The optical characterization is performed based on finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) method and our results show that the helix structure, consisting of dielectric helices arranged in a square lattice, exhibits multiple resonant peaks. The retrieved effective parameters from the complex transmittance and reflectance will be presented and the dispersion characteristics will be discussed based on various geometric parameters. The resonant frequencies can be tuned by structural parameters, and negative permeability can be achieved when the resonances are adjacent in frequencies. Depending on the geometrical arrangements, we will demonstrate the unique optical properties in an anisotropic helix nanostructure. Our analysis yields physical insight into the interaction of circularly polarized light with a three-dimensional chiral nanostructure, and provides design guidelines towards the implementation of all-dielectric photonic metamaterials.

1.

INTRODUCTION

1.

Metamaterials are artificially engineered structures that exhibit unique properties, such as negative refraction and artificial magnetism. By virtue of these properties, exciting applications, including subwavelength imaging1, subwavelength cavity resonators2, and magnetic mirrors3, can be realized. Previously metamaterials have been constructed mainly based on metals, in which negative permittivity can be readily available at some wavelength range. However, the use of metals inevitably introduces loss to the system, especially at optical frequencies, which impedes realization of high performance optical metamaterials. To solve the loss problem, all-dielectric metamaterials have emerged as an attractive alternative4. Optical properties of the dielectric metamaterials are governed by the resonance behaviors of the constituent dielectric motifs. By tuning the refractive index and geometry of the dielectrics, negative effective parameters can be achieved5.

Dielectric helix structure has been reported to display negative refraction as a photonic crystal6, and past studies suggest that by adjusting the structural parameters of a periodic array, the structure can transit from photonic crystal to metamaterial7. Moreover, helix, as a bianisotropic structure, displays cross-coupling effect between electric and magnetic field. Bianisotropy is considered a key to negative effective parameters or artificial magnetism8,9. In chiral structures, the cross-coupling effect originates from the different response to right circularly polarized (RCP) wave and to left circularly polarized (LCP) wave, and the difference can be described by a chirality parameter κ. The existence of κ implies that the effective permittivity and permeability of RCP and LCP wave have distinct values, and also opens up a new route to negative effective parameters or negative effective refractive indices.

In this report, we utilize the parameter retrieval technique to analyze the optical properties of a periodic structure consisting of dielectric helices. Effective electromagnetic parameters are evaluated by the complex transmission and reflection coefficients. The resonance responses of the structure are examined by analyzing the field distributions, and the movement of the resonance is monitored with different structural parameters of the helix. The relationship between the retrieved parameters and the structural parameters of the helix structure is discussed. Based on the analysis results, negative parameters can be obtained at some frequency ranges. In the following sections, the methods are elaborated and the effective parameters of the structure are presented and discussed.

2.

METHOD

2.

The helix structure under study is illustrated in Fig. 1(a). The structure consists of right-handed (RH) helices arranged in a 2D square lattice, as shown in Fig. 1(b). The geometrical parameters are defined by the helix radius R, pitch length p, helix spacing a, and wire radius r. In the following, the wire radius is set to be 50 nm, and the helix with one pitch is employed to eliminate unnecessary resonant peaks and avoid ambiguity in the retrieved parameters10.

3.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

3.

The optical properties of the helix structures are examined by adjusting the geometrical arrangements. When the helices are closer in space, the structure is operated as a photonic crystal, where the lowest gap is governed by the Bragg resonance6. As we increase the lattice constant and helix radius, resonance bands of the helix structure move toward lower frequencies, and the operation scenario gradually transits from the photonic crystal regime to the metamaterial regime. Fig. 2(a) shows a typical RCP transmission spectrum that depicts the resonance features of the helix structure upon the incidence of RCP light. The corresponding effective refractive index, retrieved from the method discussed in the previous section, is shown in Fig. 2(b), where the solid line is the real part and the dashed line is the imaginary part. One can see that the transmittance exhibits two valleys at 290 THz and 341 THz, representing the occurrence of resonances, denoted as P1 and P2, respectively. The corresponding real part and imaginary part of the refractive index at P1 and P2 exhibit a typical resonance behavior. To further examine the resonance characteristics, we analyze the field distributions at P1 and P2, as shown in Fig. 2(c) and 2(d), respectively. At P1, the electric field is confined along the helix wire, while at P2 the electric field is around the structure. The directions of the electric fields are denoted in the figure.

Figure 2.

(a) The transmission coefficient of RCP light and (b) the retrieved effective RCP index of the helix structure. (c)-(d) The corresponding mode profile at P1 and P2, respectively.

Previous studies have demonstrated that negative parameters can be achieved by bringing the electric and magnetic resonances closer together5. To manipulate the resonance conditions, we first monitor the variation of resonance frequencies with respect to the change of helix geometrical parameters, i.e., the lattice constant a, pitch length p, and radius R. Fig. 3(a) shows the shift of the resonance frequency of P1 and P2 while varying the lattice constant a. Both resonances exhibit a constant red shift as the lattice constant increases. This is consistent with our previous result, where the resonant mode also redshifts at a large lattice constant6. When we change the pitch length p of the helices, the two resonances display different tendencies. Fig. 3(b) shows the shift of the two resonances when a=0.8μm. The resonance frequency of P1 slightly decreases as the pitch length increases from 0.6μm to 1μm, whereas the resonance of P2 experiences a blue shift instead. It is difficult, though, to bring the two resonances even closer, as the strength of the magnetic resonance is getting weaker for a larger p. Fig. 3(c) shows the dependence of the resonant frequencies on the helix radius R. As R increases, we can see that P1 resonance displays a red-shift, while P2 resonance stays around the same frequency. When R keeps increasing, the two resonances tend to move toward each other.

Figure 3.

The electric and magnetic resonant frequencies under different (a) lattice constant, (b) pitch length, and (c) helix radius.

Based on the analysis results, we adjusted the structural parameters of the helix structure and examined the optical properties. Two designs are presented, i.e., configuration A with a=1μm, p=0.4μm, R=0.57μm in Figs. 4(a)-(d) and configuration B with a=1.1μm, p=0.4μm, R=0.6μm in Figs. 4(e)-(h). Both configurations have a relatively low p/a ratio to support strong resonances, and a high R/a ratio in order to yield resonances in close proximity.

Figure 4.

The retrieved parameters of helix metamaterial for (a)-(d) a=1μm, p=0.4μm, R=0.57μm, and (e)-(h) a=1.1μm, p=0.4μm, R=0.6μm.

Fig. 4(a) shows the RCP transmittance and reflectance of the structure with configuration A. Within the band colored in yellow, two adjacent transmission minima can be observed, indicating the resonances are close in frequency. From the transmission and reflection coefficients, the effective RCP index are calculated and displayed in Fig. 4(b), where the real part is plotted by the solid line and the imaginary part by the dashed line. The RCP effective index experiences successive drops at resonances. The retrieved permittivity and permeability are plotted in Fig. 4(c) and 4(d), where the real part is represented by the solid line and the imaginary part by the dashed line. A negative effective permittivity band, highlighted in green, and a negative effective permeability band, highlighted in yellow, are identified.

In configuration B, it is shown in Fig. 4(e) that the resonances are not as close as those in configuration A. Consider Fig. 4(f), the effective RCP index drop caused by resonance is also not as significant as in configuration A. Bands of negative parameters can also be identified in Fig. 4(g) and 4(h). (highlighted in green for negative permittivity, and in yellow for negative permeability). Comparing the retrieved parameters of the two structures, we can see that while P1 and P2 almost overlap in frequency, a more pronounced effect of negative permeability can be obtained. The results show potential of artificial magnetism in dielectric helix structures.

For the frequency bands with negative permittivities, the negative values are quite small, since they are in the off-resonance regime. The small negative effective permittivities are associated with the retrieved impedance. Fig. 5 plots the impedance of configuration B. The impedance shows a sudden -90° phase in the band of negative effective permittivity, and a +90° phase in the band of negative permeability. The -90° phase of impedance implies that the metamaterial is capacitive, giving rise to a negative effective permittivity in the region. The +90° phase suggests that the metamaterial is inductive, which results in a small negative permeability when the two resonant peaks are not close enough. Similar trend of impedance has been reported in other dielectric matamaterials14.These two bands only appear when both the lattice constant and R/p ratio are large enough. The cause of the abnormal dispersion and the manipulation of the optical properties will be investigated in our future work.

The retrieved impedance of helix metamaterial for a=1.1μm, p=0.4μm, R=0.6μm.

4.

CONCLUSION

4.

In sum, the electromagnetic response of dielectric helix metamaterials is numerically investigated. The structure features two distinctive RCP resonances. The trend of the two resonant frequencies while varying the lattice constant, pitch length, and helix radius is studied, and is utilized in designing metamaterial that exhibits negative permittivity and permeability. We demonstrated that the negative effective parameters are related to the gaps between the resonant peaks. The general design guidelines to achieve negative parameters are presented, and retrieved impedance is briefly discussed to explain the optical properties.
